##In the social sciences, what are two ethical or scientific risks of collecting data via webs craping (e.g., representativeness, privacy, terms of service, measurement error, scraping-induced missingness)? For each risk, briefly describe one practical mitigation strategy you would use in a reproducible workflow. 

Data collected via webscraping can present some challenges for researchers who wish to maintain ethical and rigorous scientific standards. For example, one ethical issue that arises from webscraping is the possibility that a researcher’s data and findings (once published) can be used for nefarious purposes. One major problem in criminology is predictive policing, which profiles certain areas (often low-income and majority-POC) as more “criminal” based on maps or police data interpreted by certain published articles as exhibiting so-and-so patterns of crime. To counteract this, a researcher interested in conducting spatial analysis of community crime or something similar could incorporate clear instructions regarding the intended purpose of the data they are using, or require others to submit official research abstracts to access their data (such as the ICPSR system hosted by UMich). One scientific risk of webscraped data is the use of convenience sampling or any form of non-random sampling, which is highly likely when any researcher is trying to access any data in the most efficient way possible. To avoid flawed sampling and research design, scholars should limit their projects to well-defined subsamples prior to actually engaging in webscraping, and be transparent with what limitations are present in the project.
